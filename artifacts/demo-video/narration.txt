The web is built for everyone. But the same page can create very different barriers for different people.

AURA is a personalized accessibility browser. It learns a person across six connected areas: visual, auditory, motor, cognitive, attention, and language support—because real needs rarely fit one label.

On any page, AURA can scan for accessibility evidence and explain how well that page fits this person. Automated WCAG checks remain factual and separate. AURA Fit adds the individual context: small text, crowded choices, difficult controls, motion, and cognitive load.

Then, one action: Make This Mine.

AURA keeps the real website underneath, but reshapes its presentation. Its private on-device planner responds immediately. A deeper semantic planner understands the page’s purpose, brings forward what matters, and explains the improvement.

AURA is also conversational. Ask, “Show me the search,” and the interface reorganizes around the real search control—not a generated imitation.

Every change is reversible. Original restores the source website instantly.

AURA does not diagnose people, and it does not replace accessible web design. It adds a personal, explainable layer of control.

Because there is no single accessible interface.

The web should adapt to the person.
